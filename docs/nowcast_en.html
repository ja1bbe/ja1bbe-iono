Kp forecast (NOAA, 3 days, 3-hour blocks): solid bars = observed or estimated, outlined bars = forecast

The forecast is issued by the NOAA Space Weather Prediction Center and is based mainly on expected CME (coronal mass ejection) arrivals and on high-speed solar wind streams that tend to recur every 27 days. CME arrival times are typically uncertain by around half a day, so treat the forecast times with some latitude. Headings are great-circle bearings from your grid square, measured from true north. If your rotator is calibrated to magnetic north, correct for your local magnetic declination. “Darkness” is the share of points along the path where the sun is at least 6° below the horizon. “Max geomagnetic latitude” uses a simple dipole approximation of the Earth’s field; over North America it is higher than the geographic latitude because the geomagnetic pole lies on that side. The aurora probability is the highest value along the path from NOAA’s OVATION model, which looks 30 to 90 minutes ahead (30% or more is shown as “crossing”, 10–30% as “edge”). Inside the auroral zone, precipitating electrons increase D- and E-layer absorption, and 160 m and 80 m signals can drop sharply. When the short path meets the auroral zone, the table also suggests a “skewed heading”: the smallest turn (up to 60° either side) for which a bent path, modelled as two great-circle legs, can reach the station without entering the zone. It is a guide to headings worth trying, not a guarantee.

Ionosphere over Kokubunji (Tokyo, Japan)

Virtual height (last 6 hours of data, km)
foF2 (last 6 hours of data, MHz)

These are ionosonde readings taken directly above Kokubunji, Tokyo, so they are most relevant to East Asia; they do not describe conditions along your own path. Virtual height is the reflection height derived from the echo delay; h’F is used when h’F2 cannot be scaled, for example at night. A sudden rise can precede a disturbance. The MUF estimate is roughly three times foF2 (an approximate value for a single hop of about 3,000 km). The values are automatically scaled and may contain errors. The public data file is updated with a delay, so the readings can be many hours old.

Source: National Institute of Information and Communications Technology (NICT), ionospheric observation data. 出典：情報通信研究機構（NICT） 電離圏観測データ https://wdc.nict.go.jp/Ionosphere/index.html

The guidance on this page summarizes general tendencies from day and night at your location, the solar flux and the geomagnetic conditions. It is not a guarantee of actual conditions. Low-band propagation also depends strongly on darkness, D-layer absorption and the noise at both ends of the path. In the end, what you actually hear and work is what counts.
